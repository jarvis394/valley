# Research plan: Valley, a self-hostable tool for building photo galleries

Vladislav Ekushev, Master's thesis, University of Oulu. Supervisor: Sohail [surname].

## Abstract

Photographers deliver their work as folders in shared drives, chat dumps or template portfolio sites. These either show photos as a flat grid or need design skills and a paid subscription. Valley is a gallery builder that tries to close this gap. It has a block-based no-code editor, "photo islands" that group a shoot into moments, and a version that photographers can host on their own free account. This thesis evaluates Valley in three studies. In the first, 12 or more participants make a gallery from the same photos in 15 minutes; it measures pride in the result, the Creativity Support Index and how far people change the starter template. In the second, 20 or more viewers compare a gallery with photo islands to a plain grid of the same kind of shoot, and are tested on what they remember. In the third, 3 photographers deploy their own Valley and use it for two real shoots, with diaries, UEQ-S and interviews. The results show whether a no-code editor and moment-based layout help photographers present their work, and whether self-hosting is realistic for them.

## Research questions

- **RQ1.** Can people without design experience use Valley's no-code editor to make a gallery they are proud of, while people with design experience can go further (low floor, high ceiling)?
- **RQ2.** Do photo islands help viewers understand and remember a shoot better than a date-sorted grid?
- **RQ3.** Can a photographer deploy their own Valley without help, and do they keep using it for real work?
- **RQ4.** Would people choose Valley over what they use now (Google Photos, Adobe Portfolio, Vigbo, Immich)? This is measured inside Studies 1 and 3, not as its own study.

## Evaluation

Full hypotheses, measures and analysis are in the [evaluation plan](evaluation-plan.md). The forms are [Form 1](forms/1-editor.md), [Form 2](forms/2-gallery-viewing.md) and [Form 3](forms/3-self-hosting.md).

| Study             | RQ       | Participants            | Method                                                                           | Main measures                                                                                               |
| ----------------- | -------- | ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. No-code editor | RQ1, RQ4 | 12 or more              | 15-minute task with the same starter gallery and photos, then a questionnaire    | Pride (5 levels), Creativity Support Index, edit distance from the editor log, comparison with current tool |
| 2. Gallery value  | RQ2      | 20 or more              | View two galleries, recall, then compare them                                    | Number of moments recalled, 1–5 comparison scales                                                           |
| 3. Self-hosting   | RQ3, RQ4 | Exactly 3 photographers | Deploy alone, then use for 2 shoots; diary form after each step; final interview | Completion, failure points, time, UEQ-S, use in shoot 2                                                     |

All forms use 5-point scales, except UEQ-S, which keeps its validated 7-point scale. Each study is piloted with 2 people first.

## Data and ethics

- The forms run on Microsoft Forms in the University of Oulu account. Names and emails are not collected.
- Answers are pseudonymous: a random code links them to the Valley logs. Study 3 uses codes P1–P3.
- Participants are 18 or older, give consent at the start, and can stop at any time.
- A GDPR [privacy notice](privacy-notice.md) is linked from every form. The University of Oulu is the controller; the legal basis is scientific research in the public interest.
- Raw data is deleted when the thesis is graded.

## Limitations

- Study 2 always shows the islands gallery first, so order and gallery content are mixed with the layout.
- 12 participants is enough to describe Study 1 but not to prove small differences between groups; effect sizes are reported.
- 3 photographers give depth, not generality.
